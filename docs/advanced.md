# Advanced data, retrieval and reader setup

CEBRA's primary interface operates on fixed retrieval trajectories. Large upstream models and reader services are separate from the CPU quick start.

## Dataset preparation

Obtain HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA and MuSiQue from their official sources linked in the README. Follow their licenses. Prepare corpora, training/calibration/test IDs and supporting-document annotations separately. The frozen evaluation configurations correspond to 1,000 calibration questions per dataset and test sizes 5,311 / 950 / 252. Public benchmark splits alone do not reconstruct those historical subsets or trajectories; this release does not claim to bundle them.

The transfer scorer used for 2WikiMultiHopQA and MuSiQue was trained on 4,811 HotpotQA validation-derived frozen trajectories, not the official training split. The HotpotQA scorer uses 14,576 2WikiMultiHopQA training questions. Preserve these bindings for comparisons.

## Optional upstream retrieval

The retained compatibility implementation uses Contriever-MSMARCO for retrieval and DeBERTa-v3-large for labeling/filtering. For baseline provenance and citation see [third-party acknowledgments](third-party.md). These components produce the trajectory format; they are not the CAES scorer.

Use a **separate** environment for the legacy upstream `requirements.txt`, which includes GPU/service dependencies and is not the validated lightweight profile. Do not install it as part of the CPU quick start. Inspect `src/conf/config.py`, model paths and current hardware before running the legacy pipeline. Its actual corpus extractor is `src/retrievers/multihop_data_extrator.py` (historical spelling preserved).

The compatibility commands are `src/efficientrag_retrieve.py` and `src/efficientrag_qa.py`; their names remain unchanged so existing experiments and imports continue to work. Retrieval needs the corpus index and trained labeler/filter checkpoints. No large checkpoints are shipped or downloaded automatically. Existing synthesized data may be reused subject to its terms; reproducing CEBRA does not require launching the original 70B synthesis pipeline from scratch.

## Downstream QA

The paper uses Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct on CUDA and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct through hosted APIs. Download/access those models under their own terms and independently configure reader services. The retained legacy QA script supports its historical model aliases; this release does **not** claim to add a ready-to-run Llama-3.3-70B provider integration.

Use `extract_final_chunks` in the CAES module to assemble the unique retained passages, then provide only the question and passages to the reader. Record prompt template, model identifier, decoding parameters, service/runtime version, and answer/judgment provenance. Keep evidence-retention metrics separate from answer EM/F1/correctness and from reader latency. API costs or model outputs are not generated by `cebra.py evaluate`.

Never commit service credentials or deployment endpoints. Keep secrets in environment variables or ignored local configuration. Do not publish full raw workspace backups as code-release assets.

## Baselines and scope

The repository retains Semantic MMR, AdaGReS, submodular packing and other research utilities under `src/evaluation/`, plus retrieval-strategy baselines. Those have their own dependencies and protocols; `cebra.py calibrate` does not silently execute or retune them. See their command help before use. The current public verification exercises the CEBRA wrapper and its unchanged core; it is not a rerun of all GPU, reader or baseline experiments.
